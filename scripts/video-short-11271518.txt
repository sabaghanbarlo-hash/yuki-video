Sometimes you don't miss the person. You miss who you were when you were with them. You miss the excitement, the routine, the messages, the feeling of being wanted. Your brain remembers the good moments and conveniently puts the bad ones in a folder labeled "Let's Not Talk About That." That's why leaving someone who hurt you can feel harder than leaving someone you never loved. You're not necessarily longing for them. You're longing for a version of your life that no longer exists. And healing begins when you stop confusing nostalgia with a reason to go back.
