Okay, be honest. How many New Year's resolutions have you made in your life?

And more importantly, how many of them have you actually completed?

Because if you're anything like me, January usually starts with the confidence of someone who's about to become the main character of their own documentary.

You buy a new notebook. You make a beautiful to-do list. You download three productivity apps. You decide that THIS is finally the year you're going to change your entire existence.

You're going to exercise every day, read 50 books, learn a new language, become financially independent, fix your sleep schedule, eat healthy, and somehow become a completely different human being by February.

And then February arrives.

Suddenly, your gym membership is just a monthly donation to a building you used to visit.

Your reading challenge is going great, except you've read the same paragraph six times.

And your productivity app? Well, it's very productive at sending you notifications that you ignore.

And just like that, another year, another failed resolution.

But what if the problem isn't that you're lazy? What if the problem is the way you're approaching personal growth in the first place?

Today, I want to introduce you to something that might actually work better than New Year's resolutions.

It's called having a theme.

Yes. Like a theme park. Except instead of spending 40 dollars on a disappointing hot dog, you're trying to improve your life.

Here's the idea.

Instead of saying, "I'm going to lose 10 kilograms this year," or "I'm going to read 52 books," you choose something broader.

For example, the Year of Health.

Or the Year of Learning.

Or the Year of Adventure.

And that's it.

No strict numbers. No ridiculous deadlines. No promise that you're going to become a completely unrecognizable person in 365 days.

Just a general direction you want your life to move toward.

Now, you might be thinking, "Okay, but isn't that a little too vague?"

And yes! That's actually the entire point.

Think about it.

Let's say you decide that this is going to be your Year of Reading.

You don't necessarily need to read one book every week. You don't need to track every single page or feel guilty because you spent Tuesday watching cat videos instead of reading Dostoevsky.

You simply want reading to become a bigger part of your life.

So one day, you're waiting for someone, and instead of opening Instagram for the 47th time, you open a book.

Another day, you have some free time, and you decide to read a few pages.

Maybe you discover that you actually enjoy audiobooks more than physical books.

Maybe you start reading academic articles instead.

Maybe you realize that you don't even enjoy reading fiction and would rather spend your time learning about psychology.

Congratulations. You're still on theme.

The point isn't to follow some perfect plan. It's to make slightly different decisions that move you in the direction you want to go.

And here's why I think this is so useful.

When you set a very specific goal, it's incredibly easy to turn the entire experience into a pass-or-fail situation.

You wanted to exercise five times a week, but you only exercised twice.

Failure.

You wanted to read 30 books, but you only read four.

Failure.

You wanted to learn French in six months, but you still can't understand what the waiter is saying.

Failure.

And suddenly, instead of appreciating the progress you've made, you're disappointed because you didn't reach some arbitrary number you decided on while experiencing a random burst of motivation in January.

But with a theme, there's no finish line you have to cross to prove that you've improved.

You're looking at the general direction of your life.

And sometimes, even moving from terrible to slightly less terrible is progress.

Let's say your theme is the Year of Health.

At first, you think that means losing weight and going to the gym.

But after a few months, you realize that your sleep schedule is a disaster, you're constantly stressed, and you haven't been taking care of your mental health.

So you start working on those things instead.

You haven't failed your theme.

You've simply discovered what health actually means to you.

And that's something I really like about this approach.

It allows you to change your mind.

Because let's be honest, the person who made your New Year's resolution in January might not be the same person you are in September.

Your interests change. Your circumstances change. You learn new things. You discover that some goals aren't even important to you anymore.

Why should you spend an entire year trying to satisfy the expectations of a version of yourself that no longer exists?

A theme gives you room to grow without constantly feeling like you're falling behind.

And there's another interesting thing that happens when you choose a theme.

You start noticing opportunities you would normally ignore.

Let's say you choose the Year of Novelty.

Maybe your daily routine has become incredibly repetitive.

You wake up, check your phone, go to work or university, come home, scroll through social media, watch something, and repeat.

You're basically an NPC with a very limited dialogue selection.

But now, because you've chosen novelty as your theme, you start paying attention to opportunities to do something different.

You try a new café.

You take a different route home.

You start learning something you've always been curious about.

You talk to someone new.

You visit a place you've never been.

None of these things has to be particularly impressive.

You don't need to suddenly move to Japan, climb Mount Everest, or become a mysterious international spy.

You just need to introduce a little more variety into your everyday life.

And eventually, those small decisions start changing your experiences.

Your theme becomes like a little reminder following you around.

Whenever you reach a decision, it quietly asks, "Hey, remember what we're trying to do this year?"

And sometimes, that's enough to make you choose differently.

Now, if you're wondering how to choose your own theme, I think there are three things worth considering.

First, think about what you feel is missing from your life.

Do you want more adventure? More discipline? More creativity? More connection? More peace?

Second, don't make it too specific.

Remember, you're choosing a direction, not creating a military operation.

And third, choose something that actually excites you.

This part is important.

Don't choose a theme just because it sounds impressive.

You don't need to declare the Year of Financial Optimization if the phrase makes you want to fall asleep immediately.

Choose something that makes you feel something.

Something that reminds you of the person you want to become.

It could be the Year of Confidence.

The Year of Creativity.

The Year of Independence.

The Year of Curiosity.

The Year of Friendship.

The Year of Discipline.

Or maybe even the Year of Doing Things Scared.

There isn't a correct answer.

And one last thing.

You don't even have to make it a year.

Honestly, a year is a pretty long time.

You can choose a theme for a season, a few months, or even a shorter period of your life.

Maybe this winter is your Winter of Learning.

You spend a few months developing a skill, exploring new interests, and challenging yourself intellectually.

Then spring comes, and perhaps you want something different.

That's perfectly fine.

Your life isn't a video game where you have to complete every quest in a specific order before unlocking the next level.

You can change direction.

You can take breaks.

You can start again.

And most importantly, you don't have to wait until January 1st to become a little more intentional about your life.

You can start on a random Tuesday.

You can start in the middle of July.

You can start after spending three hours watching videos of people renovating tiny houses.

It doesn't matter.

So, if you're watching this and you've been feeling stuck, or you've been disappointed in yourself because you haven't achieved everything you planned, maybe stop trying to completely reinvent your life overnight.

Instead, ask yourself one simple question:

What do I want more of in my life?

And turn the answer into a theme.

Because you don't need to become a completely different person by the end of the year.

You just need to start making little decisions that take you closer to the life you actually want to live.

And who knows?

Maybe by the end of the year, you'll look back and realize that all those tiny, seemingly insignificant decisions added up to something pretty amazing.
