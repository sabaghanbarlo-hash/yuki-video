Everyone is watching you. Right? Wrong.
In a famous experiment, students wore an embarrassing T-shirt and walked into a room full of people. They guessed about half of the room would notice.
The real number was closer to one in four.
Psychologists call it the spotlight effect. You are the main character of your own life, so you feel like everyone sees every awkward moment.
But everyone else is busy starring in their own story, worrying about their own shirt.
So next time you trip, stumble over a word, or send the wrong text, relax. Almost nobody noticed. And those who did forgot by lunch.
