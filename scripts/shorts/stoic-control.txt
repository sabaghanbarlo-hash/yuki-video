The Stoics had one trick for bad days.
Epictetus, a former slave turned philosopher, taught it two thousand years ago. Some things are up to you: your thoughts, your choices, your effort.
Everything else is not. Other people, the weather, traffic, the past, what they think of you.
When something goes wrong, ask one question. Is this up to me?
If yes, act. If no, let it go. Worrying about the weather has never changed the weather.
Marcus Aurelius wrote reminders like this to himself every morning, and he ran an empire.
You control less than you think. And that is exactly why it is so freeing.
