You are in a loud room. Fifty conversations at once. And somehow, you hear your own name across the room.
How?
In 1953, scientist Colin Cherry called it the cocktail party effect. Your brain is always filtering sound, even when you are not listening on purpose.
Most voices get tuned out. But some things break through: your name, a danger, a word you care about.
Your attention works like a bouncer at a club. It checks every sound at the door, and only the important ones get in.
So no, you are not imagining it. Your brain really was listening the whole time.
